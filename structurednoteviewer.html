Executive Summary

This lecture explores the fundamental contradiction at the heart of quantum mechanics: the wave-particle duality. It explains how entities like electrons and photons exhibit properties of both particles (localized mass) and waves (interference patterns). The core takeaway is that the behavior observed depends entirely on the measurement apparatus used, leading to Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle.

Key Concepts

Photoelectric Effect

The phenomenon where light shining on a metal surface causes the emission of electrons. This proved light behaves as particles (photons) rather than just waves.

Double Slit Experiment

A classic demonstration showing that light and matter can display characteristics of both classically defined waves and particles; it shows the fundamental probabilistic nature of quantum mechanical phenomena.
